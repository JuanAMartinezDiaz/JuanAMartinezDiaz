## Corrections made while applying the exercises

- Deployer duties are addressed in Article 26, not Articles 28–29; the latter concern notifying authorities and conformity assessment bodies.
- Article 17 concerns quality management. Article 72 specifically addresses provider post-market monitoring.
- The Commission's current timeline lists 2 December 2027 for Annex III high-risk rules and 2 August 2028 for Annex I high-risk rules. The source exercise's broad 2026 high-risk applicability assumption is not carried forward. Transitional provisions and individual facts still require review.
- Human review alone does not remove high-risk status. Article 6 exceptions need a supported analysis; profiling in an Annex III use case prevents reliance on that exception.
- Transparency obligations can coexist with high-risk classification. “Limited risk” is not used as a mutually exclusive legal clearance.
- A documentation packet does not establish conformity. Provider evidence, deployer controls, applicable assessment, declaration, registration and operational readiness are separate questions.

## Interpretation boundaries

This is an educational application to stated fictional facts. Internal severity scores, time targets, pass criteria, retention proposals, and committee roles are design choices, not statutory standards. Any GDPR, employment, banking, consumer-protection, or US-law applicability requires a separate jurisdictional assessment. The portfolio does not transfer EU duties to US-only activity by analogy. ISO standards are not presented as assessed or certified.

Refresh trigger: changed law or official guidance; changed intended purpose, data, model, vendor, geography, integration, or decision authority. Legal owns applicability review in the fictional operating model. A change blocks reliance on stale release evidence until its impact is assessed.
