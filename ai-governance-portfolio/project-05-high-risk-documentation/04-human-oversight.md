# 04 — Human oversight

**Synthetic operating procedure.** Article 14 informs provider design; Article 26 informs deployer responsibilities. Proposed overseers are trained HR reviewers with explicit authority and time to disagree, refer, and stop use.

Before seeing the AI ranking, a reviewer records an assessment against job-relevant criteria. The interface then shows the AI result alongside source material and limitations. Reviewers can inspect all applicants, including those omitted from a proposed shortlist. A disagreement requires a reason and may trigger a second reviewer; agreement alone is not evidence of independent review.

Reviewers must stop reliance on the system for unsupported language, missing material information, unexplained contradictions, suspected manipulation or an unavailable evidence record. The manual process remains accessible. An operations manager can suspend the integration without vendor consent, subject to a tested technical mechanism. Restart follows the release gate.

Proposed competence exercise: twenty cases including five deliberately misleading rankings, incomplete records, accommodation requests and an injected instruction. A reviewer must identify all five intentionally misleading rankings, locate source evidence and demonstrate escalation. Failure leads to retraining or redesign, not automatic approval after attendance.

Appraisal and workload measures must not punish justified overrides. Review override patterns for rubber-stamping, unexplained disparity and excessive workload, with privacy protections. A zero-override rate is a signal to investigate, not proof of model quality.

Evidence state: procedure designed; interface, permissions, training, stop capability and exercises not implemented or run here. No person is represented as an authenticated approver in the synthetic packet.
